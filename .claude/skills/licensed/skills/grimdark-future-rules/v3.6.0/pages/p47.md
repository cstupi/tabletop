# Grimdark Future v3.6.0 — page 47

USING UNIT PSYCHOLOGY
Unit psychology rules change how units behave as they are put
through continuous fighting, making them less predictable.
Pick any of the following:
• Intimidation
• Frenzy
• Panic
• Retreating
• Suppression
INTIMIDATION
When units from opposite sides are contesting the same objective,
compare the total point value of all units from each side, where
units with half or less of their total size or Tough value (for single-
model units) are worth half of their point value (rounding down).
If one side has a total that is at least 50% higher than the other,
then it seizes the objective.
Example: Army A has 200pts worth of units contesting an objective,
but they are at half or less of their total size value (halving their worth
to 100pts), whilst army B has 150pts worth of units contesting the
same objective, so army B seizes it.
FRENZY
When a unit fully destroys an enemy unit in melee or shooting, roll
one die. On a 1, the unit is frenzied.
When activated, frenzied units must either shoot at the closest
available enemy (moving as needed to get as many of its models
into range as possible), or must charge the closest enemy unit, and
if neither is possible, then the unit ends its activation. Frenzied
units stop being frenzied at the end of their activation.
PANIC
When a unit would be Shaken, roll one die. On a 1, the unit panics
instead of being Shaken.
When a unit that is panicking is activated, the opponent may
control it for that activation, and then it stops panicking at the end
of its activation.
RETREATING
At the end of an activation in which a unit has taken wounds from
shooting, it must move X” directly away from the shooting unit,
where X is 1” for each wound it took.
When a unit is Shaken after losing a melee, it must move X” directly
away from the enemy unit it fought, where X is 2” for each wound it
took.
If a retreat move can’t be completed because the unit would hit an
obstacle or unit, then it takes D3 wounds and stops within 1” of the
obstacle or unit. If a retreat move would bring off the table, then
it’s removed from play as destroyed.
SUPPRESSION
Units get 1 suppression marker when one of the following things
happens during the game:
• After they fought in melee.
• When a friendly unit within 6” is destroyed or Routs after
failing a Morale Test.
• Units may also willingly get 1 suppression marker to add
+2” to their Advance or +4” to their Rush/Charge moves.
There is no limit to how many suppression markers a unit can take
or have during the game.
For each suppression marker:
• Units move -1” when using Advance, and -2” when using
Rush/Charge actions
• Units get -1 to hit rolls when shooting or in melee
• Units get -1 to Morale Test rolls
When a unit takes n suppression marker that brings it to a total of 3
markers or more, then it must take a Morale Test, and if failed, then
the unit is Shaken, even if it didn’t take wounds.
When a unit that has suppression markers is activated, it must take
as many Morale Tests as markers on it, ignoring the penalty of -1 to
Morale Test rolls from suppression. For each success you may
remove one marker from the unit, and once you are done you may
activate it normally.
Units with suppression markers may use a Rest action by staying
idle during their activation, which removes all markers at the end
of the round. Note that Shaken units may use Rest actions to
remove all suppression as well as stop being Shaken.
When a Hero is activated, you may remove 1 suppression marker
from the Hero and their unit, and from all other friendly units
within 12” (this doesn’t require any rolls).
