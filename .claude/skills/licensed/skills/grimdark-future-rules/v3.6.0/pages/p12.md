# Grimdark Future v3.6.0 — page 12

OPEN TERRAIN
Examples: Grass Fields, Dirt Roads, Streets, etc.
Any surface that is not specifically defined as a type of terrain (like
forests, buildings, rivers, etc.) counts as open terrain.
Open terrain does not have any special rules, and any rules that
affect terrain do not apply to open terrain.
IMPASSABLE TERRAIN
Examples: Mountains, Canyons, Deep Water, etc.
Any surface that would stop models from moving through it, as
well as gaps over 1” wide, count as Impassable terrain.
Units may not move through Impassable terrain, unless they have
any rules that allow them to ignore it. Note that units may move
across gaps up to 1” wide as if they were flat ground, but may not
end their move partially overhanging gaps or terrain.
SIGHT BLOCKING TERRAIN
Examples: Walls, Buildings, Rocks, etc.
Any piece of terrain that models can’t see or shoot through counts
as Sight Blocking terrain.
Units may not draw line of sight through Sight Blocking terrain,
unless they have any rules that allow them to ignore it.
COVER TERRAIN
Examples: Forests, Ruins, Barricades, etc.
Terrain features that models can hide in or behind, or that could
stop projectiles, count as Cover terrain.
If the majority of models in a unit are fully inside a piece of Cover
terrain or behind a sight blocker (for multi-model units), or that are
mostly inside Cover terrain or behind sight blockers (for single-
model units), they get +1 to Defense rolls when blocking hits from
shooting attacks.
Note that since models can’t see through the perimeter of other
units (friendly or enemy), they are sight blockers that can provide
other units with cover.
DIFFICULT TERRAIN
Examples: Woods, Mud, Rivers, etc.
Terrain features that hinder a model’s movement, or force them to
slow down, count as Difficult terrain.
If any model in a unit moves in or through Difficult terrain at any
point of its move, then all models in the unit may not move more
than 6” for that movement.
DANGEROUS TERRAIN
Examples: Quicksand, Lava Pools, Deadly Vegetation, etc.
Terrain features that could harm models, or outright kill them,
count as Dangerous terrain.
If any model in a unit moves into or through Dangerous terrain, or
activates in it, then the entire unit must take a Dangerous Terrain
Test.
To take a Dangerous Terrain Test, roll as many dice as the number
of wounds it would take to fully destroy the unit, and for each
result is 1 the unit takes one wound.
DENSE TERRAIN
Examples: Thick Vegetation, Open Buildings, Steamy Areas, etc.
Terrain features that are densely pack and that would be Difficult
to see through count as dense terrain.
Units may draw line of sight into and out of dense terrain, but not
through it to targets on the other side.
UNSTABLE TERRAIN
Examples: Crumbling Buildings, Rubble, Traps, etc.
Terrain features that could be harmful if not moved through
carefully count as unstable terrain.
If any model in a unit moves over 6” when moving into or through
unstable terrain, then it counts as Dangerous terrain.
STEEP TERRAIN
Examples: Hills, Slopes, Tall Stairs, etc.
Terrain features that require extra effort to get on top of, but that
are easy to get down from, count as steep terrain.
Units moving onto steep terrain count it as being in Difficult terrain,
but units moving off it count it as open terrain.
CLIMBABLE TERRAIN
Examples: Scalable Walls, Rooftops, Cliffs, etc.
Terrain features that are over 3” tall count as Impassable terrain,
but any terrain piece that is up to 3” tall may be climbed as part of
a unit’s move (units may not end their move mid-climb). If not all
models in a unit can fit at the top, then a coherency of 3” is allowed
to models at different elevations.
Note that short terrain up to 1” tall may be ignored for the
purposes of movement (instead of having to climb up 1” and down
1” to cross it).
